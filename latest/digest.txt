📚 Daily Knowledge — September 21, 2026

🌐 Top World News
• Ukraine launched its largest-ever drone attack on Moscow overnight into Sept. 20, striking the Kapotnya oil refinery that supplies about 40% of the capital's fuel; Moscow's mayor called it the biggest such strike of the war, and regional officials reported 3 killed and more than 20 injured, with the attack landing on the final day of Russia's regional elections held partly in Ukraine's annexed territories.
• Canada's oil-rich province of Alberta held a weekend vote on a possible referendum to separate from Canada, a move that could ripple through North American energy politics given Alberta's role as a major supplier of oil and natural gas to the United States.
• President Trump said the U.S. has reached a deal with Denmark to build a "large" permanent American military presence in Greenland while the territory stays under Danish sovereignty; the pact reportedly bars non-NATO bases and blocks Chinese or Russian investment, with formal signing by all three governments expected soon.

💻 Technology
• OpenAI's global policy chief confirmed the company has spent weeks coordinating with rivals Anthropic and Google DeepMind on AI safety, following Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei's essay urging the industry to "pace the frontier" and avoid catastrophic risk.
• OpenAI shipped GPT-6 Astra, a frontier model the company says meets the "Critical" cybersecurity capability threshold under its Preparedness Framework, even as security researchers reported successfully exploiting Anthropic's newly released Claude Opus 5 within hours — in contrast to the prior Opus 4.8, which had resisted the same attacks across multiple sessions.
• Trump said he plans to appoint an "AI czar" and stand up a new federal "AI Force," modeled on the Space Force, to oversee the AI industry.

🔬 Science: Caltech engineers built a nanoscale silicon metasurface device that can redirect a beam of light in just 74 femtoseconds using a second light beam, a potential building block for ultrafast optical computing.

📜 History: On September 21, 1949, Mao Zedong proclaimed the founding of the People's Republic of China in Beijing — one of the 20th century's most consequential political transformations, still shaping global affairs 77 years later.

🤔 Philosophy: Debate is intensifying this year around "limitarianism," the view that there is a moral ceiling on personal wealth, with philosophers like Kenneth Silver examining how extreme fortunes affect fairness, democracy, and social trust.

🌍 Geography: A Manhattan-sized iceberg has calved from Greenland's Petermann Glacier as fractures weakened by years of warming finally gave way, with scientists warning two more large sections could soon break off, potentially removing about 22% of the glacier.

💰 Economics: The U.S. Federal Reserve, under new Chair Kevin Warsh, raised its benchmark rate a quarter point to 3.75%–4.00% on September 16 to fight inflation running above 3% for the first time this year, and markets now expect further hikes into 2027.

🧠 Psychology: The largest-ever genetic study of borderline personality disorder identified 11 genome regions linked to the condition, revealing shared genetic risk with PTSD, depression, and ADHD.

🎨 Art & Literature: The 2026 Booker Prize shortlist will be revealed Sept. 22 in London, narrowed from a 13-book longlist by a judging panel chaired by classicist Mary Beard.

🔭 Astronomy & Space: Astronomers have confirmed Elias 2-24 b, a Jupiter-sized planet less than a million years old, as the youngest known planet yet discovered.

🧬 Biology & Evolution: Paleontologists in Brazil identified a new giant dinosaur species roughly 20 meters long that lived 120 million years ago, revealing surprising evolutionary ties to a distantly related Spanish dinosaur.

🗺️ World Cultures & Languages: About 44% of the world's roughly 7,159 living languages are now classified as endangered, with Oceania home to the highest concentration — a crisis linguists are racing to document, from Papua New Guinea's Ende to Louisiana Creole.

➗ Mathematics: Columbia mathematician Mehtaab Sawhney and Oxford's Ben Green proved there are infinitely many prime solutions to p² + 4q² where both p and q are themselves prime, resolving a long-standing open problem in number theory.

⚖️ Politics & Law: The U.S. Supreme Court kept in place a block on a Trump-backed rule that would have restricted mail-in ballots ahead of the 2026 midterms, over dissents from Justices Thomas and Alito.

🎵 Music: Heavy metal veterans Anthrax released their 12th studio album, "Cursum Perficio," on September 18, part of a wave of major rock releases this month alongside Kasabian's "Act III" and Luke Bryan's "Signs."
