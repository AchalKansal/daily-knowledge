📚 Daily Knowledge — September 10, 2026

🌐 Top World News
• The U.S. military struck IRGC targets across Iran — including sites on Qeshm Island, Bandar Abbas, Chabahar, Jask and Sirik — after Iran attempted attacks on commercial shipping in the Strait of Hormuz and on U.S. troops in the region, part of an escalating campaign to knock out Iran's radar and missile capabilities near the strait.
• Thieves stole two Renoir paintings worth roughly €2.2 million ("Madame Colonna Romano" and "Jeune femme au puits") from the Musée Renoir in Cagnes-sur-Mer on the French Riviera, dropping two other canvases in the garden as police gave chase; French authorities have opened an organized-crime investigation.
• Sri Lankan MP Namal Rajapaksa was arrested over alleged corruption tied to SriLankan Airlines' 2013 Airbus purchase deal, the latest legal action against the politically prominent Rajapaksa family.

💻 Technology
• U.S. agencies accused Chinese AI labs of siphoning American AI model intelligence at industrial scale, escalating tensions over intellectual property just as Chinese factories showcase new humanoid robots and Beijing tightens IPO rules for robotics firms.
• Google patched its seventh actively exploited Chrome zero-day of 2026, underscoring the browser's status as a top target for real-world attacks this year.
• DeepSeek quietly opened a limited beta for V4.1 Flash, an interim model with a new architecture and native multimodal support, capped at 20 concurrent requests per account.

🔬 Science: UC San Diego researchers showed that a cellular enzyme can accurately read a synthetic eight-letter genetic alphabet, doubling the four letters (A, T, C, G) used by all known life on Earth.

📜 History: On this day in 1813, U.S. Captain Oliver Hazard Perry led nine American ships to victory over a British squadron at the Battle of Lake Erie during the War of 1812 — the first time an entire British naval squadron had been defeated and captured.

🤔 Philosophy: The Philosopher's Annual, which anthologizes the year's best philosophy papers, released its Volume 45 covering the literature of 2025, with Daniel Singer taking over as editor from longtime steward Patrick Grim.

🌍 Geography: East Antarctica gained roughly 695 billion tons of ice between 2021 and 2023, a snowfall-driven rebound that temporarily slowed the continent's long-term decline, researchers reported this month.

💰 Economics: With inflation running about a full percentage point above what forecasters expected a year ago, the Federal Reserve heads into its pivotal September 15-16 meeting under an unusually public pressure campaign from the Trump administration to hold or cut rates rather than hike.

🧠 Psychology: A major new study found that adults with depression show disrupted production of new neurons in the hippocampus, a deficit that may weaken the brain's ability to distinguish new experiences from painful memories.

🎨 Art & Literature: Jersey-based self-taught artist and writer Adam Perchard won the inaugural Hogarth Prize for his work "Leviathan," earning £3,000 and a solo exhibition at Hogarth's House in Chiswick next spring.

🔭 Astronomy & Space: NASA scientists spotted a giant ten-sided pattern circling Saturn's south pole, giving the ringed planet an unexpected southern counterpart to its famous northern hexagon.

🧬 Biology & Evolution: Geneticists reported that modern humans carry DNA from two mysterious extinct human lineages beyond Neanderthals and Denisovans, hinting at encounters with populations still unidentified in the fossil record.

🗺️ World Cultures & Languages: UNESCO convened a regional forum in Central Africa and the Great Lakes today to strengthen a "Culture of Peace," part of its broader push — alongside a new Global Action Plan for Indigenous languages — to stem the loss of up to 1,500 languages projected to disappear by century's end.

➗ Mathematics: Computer scientists achieved the first improvement in 30 years on how to fairly divide a set of objects between two groups, cracking a longstanding open problem in combinatorics.

⚖️ Politics & Law: Missouri's Supreme Court ruled the state's newly GOP-redrawn congressional map cannot be used in this fall's midterms, ordering a November referendum on the map while the old district lines stay in effect for now.

🎵 Music: Anthrax's twelfth studio album "Cursum Perficio" and Luke Bryan's "Signs" both arrive September 18, while Joy Division's sprawling 14-disc live box set "Eternal (Live)" also lands later this month.
